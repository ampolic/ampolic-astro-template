## Running, but not cooling

It's the hottest afternoon of the month, the AC is humming along, and the air from the vents is room temperature at best. Before you book a service call, there are a handful of checks that solve this more often than you'd guess. Most take a couple of minutes and cost nothing.

Work through them in order. If you get to the end and the air is still warm, then it's time to call, and you can tell us what you've already ruled out.

## Start at the thermostat

Confirm the obvious first. The thermostat should be set to "cool," not "heat" or "fan only," and the temperature set below the current room reading. If the fan is set to "on," the blower runs even when the system isn't cooling, which pushes room-temperature air and can feel like a failure when nothing's wrong. Set the fan to "auto" and see if cold air follows.

If it's a battery thermostat, weak batteries can leave it powered enough to display but too weak to signal the system. Fresh batteries are a five-minute test.

## Check the filter and the airflow

A badly clogged filter is a leading cause of weak cooling. When airflow drops, the indoor coil can get so cold it freezes into a block of ice, and a frozen coil blows warm. If you pull the filter and it's gray with dust, replace it, switch the system to fan-only for an hour to thaw any ice, then try cooling again.

While you're at it, walk to the outdoor unit. It needs to breathe. Grass clippings, cottonwood fluff, and leaves packed against the coil choke it, and so does anything stacked within a couple of feet. Clear it and hose the outside of the coil gently if it's dirty.

<Callout type="warning" title="Ice on the lines means shut it off">
  If you find ice on the refrigerant lines or the indoor coil, turn the cooling
  off and run the fan to thaw it. Running a frozen system can damage the
  compressor, the most expensive part to replace. If it freezes again after
  thawing, that's a refrigerant or airflow fault for a technician.
</Callout>

## Check the breaker and the drain

An outdoor unit that's silent while the indoor fan runs often means a tripped breaker. Air conditioners can trip the outdoor circuit separately, so check the panel and reset it once. If it trips again, stop, since a repeat trip is an electrical fault, not a nuisance.

Some systems also shut down cooling when the condensate drain backs up and a safety switch senses the full pan. Clearing the drain line can bring it back.

## When it's time to call

If the thermostat is right, the filter is clean, the outdoor unit is clear and running, the breaker holds, and the air is still warm, the likely culprits are low refrigerant, a failed capacitor, or a compressor issue, and those need tools and training. Call us with what you've checked, and we'll come ready.
