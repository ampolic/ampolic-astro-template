## The sticker price isn't the real price

A new high-efficiency system is a real expense, but the number on the quote is rarely what you actually pay. Between federal tax credits, utility rebates, and state programs, a well-chosen upgrade can come with thousands of dollars in help. The catch is that the programs are scattered, and you have to know to ask.

Here's the plain-language version of what's available to Front Range homeowners, and how the pieces stack.

## Federal tax credits

The federal government offers tax credits for qualifying high-efficiency equipment installed in your home. Heat pumps carry the largest credit (a percentage of the project cost up to an annual cap), and high-efficiency furnaces and central air conditioners qualify for smaller amounts.

Two things to keep straight. First, a tax credit reduces what you owe at filing time; it isn't cash back at purchase. Second, the equipment has to meet specific efficiency thresholds to qualify, so the model matters. We'll flag which systems on a quote actually clear the bar.

## Utility rebates

This is where the fastest money usually is. Xcel Energy, which serves much of the Boulder area, runs rebate programs for high-efficiency furnaces, air conditioners, and heat pumps, and these are typically paid as a direct rebate rather than a tax credit, so you see the benefit sooner.

Rebate amounts and qualifying equipment change from year to year, and sometimes mid-year, so the figure that mattered last season may have moved. Always confirm the current offer before you sign, and check whether your specific utility has its own program on top.

## State and local programs

Colorado has added its own incentives for electrification and efficiency, including support aimed at heat pumps as the state pushes to move homes off gas. These stack with federal and utility programs in many cases, which is how the savings add up to real money.

Because these programs open, close, and change funding, treat any specific number as something to verify at the time you buy rather than a promise.

## Making it simple

You don't have to become an expert in incentive programs to benefit from them. The practical path is to get a quote for a system that qualifies, then have your contractor identify which credits and rebates apply and handle the utility paperwork. Most of the utility side we can file on your behalf.

The one rule worth remembering: confirm every program's current terms before you commit, because the details shift. When you're ready to look at a system, we'll lay out which incentives apply to it and what they're worth this year, so the price you compare is the price you'll actually pay.
