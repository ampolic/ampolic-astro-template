## What MERV actually measures

Stand in the filter aisle and the numbers are everywhere: MERV 8, MERV 11, MERV 13, and a wall of brand names on top. MERV stands for Minimum Efficiency Reporting Value, and it measures one thing: how well a filter traps particles. The scale runs from 1 to 16 for home filters, and a higher number catches smaller particles.

That sounds like more is always better. It isn't, and the reason is airflow.

## Why higher isn't always better

A filter puts a barrier in the path of your air. The finer that barrier, the harder your blower has to work to pull air through it. Push it too far and you starve the system: airflow drops, the furnace can overheat, the AC coil can freeze, and your energy use climbs even as comfort falls.

Most homes are designed around a filter in the MERV 8 to 13 range. Go much denser than the system was built for, especially in a one-inch slot, and you can do more harm than good. The trick is matching the filter to what your equipment can move.

## Picking the right one

Here's how the tiers shake out for a typical home.

MERV 8 is the baseline. It captures dust, lint, and pollen, protects the equipment, and flows easily. It's fine for a home without allergy concerns.

MERV 11 is the sweet spot for most people. It adds finer dust, pet dander, and more pollen while still flowing well in a standard system. If someone in the house has mild allergies, start here.

MERV 13 catches smoke, fine particulates, and many bacteria-sized particles. It's the most a typical one-inch slot should carry, and only if your system has the airflow for it. It's the tier worth reaching for during wildfire-smoke season on the Front Range.

Above MERV 13 you're into territory that usually needs a dedicated media cabinet or a system built for it. Forcing that density into a standard slot backfires.

## Thickness changes the math

A four-inch media filter changes everything. Because it has far more surface area than a one-inch filter, it can hit MERV 13 without choking airflow, and it lasts six months to a year instead of one to three. If your system has a media cabinet, use it. If it doesn't, adding one is one of the better air-quality upgrades available.

## The short answer

For most Boulder-area homes, a MERV 11 one-inch filter changed every one to three months is the right call.
