## The five-minute version

A tune-up isn't a formality. For $150–$200, you get a technician checking 30+ components, spotting wear before it becomes failure, and keeping your system running at peak efficiency. That $200 investment often prevents a $2,000 breakdown and saves you 10–15% on energy bills. Over a system's 15-year life, that's thousands of dollars in your pocket.

## What we actually check

**Refrigerant charge.** Low refrigerant means your AC works harder, uses more power, and strains the compressor — the most expensive component. We verify the charge is correct. Too low? We'll find the leak, fix it, and top it off.

**Electrical connections.** Loose wires overheat, burn, and fail. We tighten every connection and test for voltage drop that signals trouble ahead.

**Blower and fan motors.** Worn bearings get louder, work harder, and eventually seize. We lubricate, listen, and tell you if replacement is coming soon.

**Condensate drain.** A clogged drain backs up water and damages your air handler. We clear it and check the pan for rust or deterioration.

**Thermostat calibration.** A thermostat even 1°F off costs you real money in overshooting or undershooting. We verify accuracy and make small adjustments.

## The efficiency bonus

A well-maintained system uses 10–15% less energy than a neglected one. In Colorado, that's savings of $15–$30 per month — $180–$360 per year. Over a decade, that's $1,800–$3,600 in lower electric bills from a $300–$400 investment in tune-ups.

## Spring and fall, every year

We recommend maintenance before AC season (April–May) and before heating season (September–October). These visits catch the seasonal startup problems: leaks, thermostats behaving strangely, capacitors about to fail. Get ahead of the heat and cold, and you'll never be without comfort.
