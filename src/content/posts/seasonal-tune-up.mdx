## The short version

A tune-up isn't a formality. For $150 to $200, a technician checks more than 30 components, catches wear before it turns into a failure, and keeps the system running efficiently. That visit often heads off a $2,000 breakdown and trims 10 to 15% off your energy bill. Across a 15-year system life, that adds up to thousands of dollars.

## What we check

We check the refrigerant charge first. When it runs low, the AC works harder, draws more power, and strains the compressor, which is the priciest part to replace. If it's low, we find the leak, fix it, and top the charge back up.

We go through the electrical connections next. Loose wires overheat and eventually fail, so we tighten every terminal and watch for the voltage drop that warns of trouble ahead.

The blower and fan motors get a look and a listen. Worn bearings grow louder and drag before they seize, so we lubricate them and tell you if a motor is near the end.

We clear the condensate drain, since a clogged one backs up water into the air handler, and we check the pan for rust while we're there.

Last is the thermostat. Even a reading that's off by a single degree costs you money as the system overshoots and undershoots, so we confirm it's accurate and adjust it.

## The efficiency payoff

A well-kept system runs 10 to 15% leaner than a neglected one. In Colorado that's about $15 to $30 a month, or $180 to $360 a year. Over a decade it comes to $1,800 to $3,600 in lower bills, against $300 to $400 a year in tune-ups.

## Spring and fall, every year

Book maintenance before AC season in April or May, and again before heating season in September or October. Those visits catch the problems that show up at startup: a small leak, a thermostat acting up, a capacitor about to quit. Handle them early and you won't get caught without heat or cooling when you need it most.

Schedule a tune-up whenever it suits you. You'll leave with a full report and a straight answer on what, if anything, to do next.
