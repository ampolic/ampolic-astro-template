## Size matters — and load calculation matters more

The most common HVAC mistake is oversizing: selling a 4-ton system to a 3-ton home because "bigger is better." Wrong. An oversized system short-cycles (runs in bursts), wasting energy, failing to dehumidify in summer, and wearing out faster.

The right size comes from a *load calculation*: measuring square footage, insulation, window area, orientation, local climate, and occupancy. We use Manual J calculations to nail your home's true heating and cooling demand. This takes 2 hours; it's worth every minute.

## SEER: efficiency and your electric bill

SEER (Seasonal Energy Efficiency Ratio) is the AC efficiency standard. Higher SEER = lower summer bills.

- **SEER 13–14**: Baseline. Meets code. Older homes, tight budgets.
- **SEER 16–17**: Sweet spot. Good efficiency, reasonable cost, 10–15% savings vs. baseline.
- **SEER 20+**: Premium efficiency. High upfront cost, but 20%+ savings for homes running AC heavily.

Colorado summers are hot but not long. Most homeowners save money at SEER 16–17. If you run AC heavily or plan to stay 15+ years, SEER 20 pencils out.

## Heat pump vs. furnace vs. hybrid

**Furnace + AC**: Traditional split system. Separate equipment for heating and cooling. Simple, reliable, efficient if properly sized. Good for homes with ductwork already in place.

**Heat pump**: One unit heats and cools. Works by moving heat (even cold air has heat to move). Highly efficient in moderate climates. In deep Colorado cold (below –10°F), efficiency drops. But most Boulder winters never hit that.

**Hybrid**: Furnace + heat pump. Runs the heat pump for mild weather, switches to the furnace for deep cold. Best efficiency in variable climates. Higher upfront cost, but shines in Colorado's variable winters.

## Brand and warranty

Buy from a manufacturer with a solid Colorado track record:

- **Carrier**: Reliable, mid-range price, good service network.
- **Lennox**: Premium efficiency options, solid warranty, higher cost.
- **York**: Good value, solid reliability, less common.
- **Trane**: Commercial-grade durability, premium pricing.

Warranty typically covers parts for 5–10 years and labor for 1–5 years. Longer labor warranty is worth the premium if you're keeping the system 15+ years.

## The real cost: installation + maintenance

The equipment is 40–50% of the job. The other 50%? Installation, ductwork, electrical work, permits, and commissioning. A cheap system installed poorly beats a premium system installed poorly. We'll recommend quality equipment AND professional installation.

And remember: your system is only as good as its maintenance. Two annual tune-ups prevent 80% of failures and keep efficiency high. Budget $300–$400 per year in maintenance, and your system will outlive expectations.
